Concepts

Route 53 and hosted zones

Route 53 is AWS's DNS service. It answers the question “what is the address for this name?”

  • A hosted zone holds all the records for one domain.
  • A public zone answers the internet. A private zone answers only inside your VPCs.
  • It is global: draw it outside every Region.
  • It only answers lookups. User traffic never flows through it.

Record types

  • A — name to an IPv4 address. AAAA is the same for IPv6.
  • CNAME — name to another name. Not allowed at the top of the zone (example.com itself).
  • Alias — a Route 53 feature: an A record that points at an AWS resource and follows its changing IPs.
  • MX for mail, TXT for verification, NS for the zone's name servers.

Health checks

Route 53 can test a target and leave it out of answers when it fails.

  • For an alias to a load balancer, turn on Evaluate target health; Route 53 then uses the load balancer's own view of its targets.
  • For anything else, create a health check that requests a URL every 10 or 30 seconds.
  • Failover routing needs a health check on the primary, or it never fails over.

TTL

Every answer says how long it may be cached. Until that time is up, the resolver does not ask again.

  • Long TTL: fewer lookups, but changes and failovers take longer to reach users.
  • Short TTL: fast changes, more queries.
  • Alias records to load balancers use 60 seconds, so a failover reaches most users within a minute or two.

Check yourself

Alias record or CNAME: what is the difference?

An alias is a Route 53 A record that points at an AWS resource, tracks its changing IP addresses, works at the zone apex and is free to query. A CNAME maps a name to any other name, is not allowed at the apex, costs a second lookup and is charged.

You want example.com, without www, to reach your load balancer. Which record?

An alias A record. A CNAME is not permitted at the zone apex, and a plain A record needs fixed IP addresses, which a load balancer does not have.

Does the user's HTTPS request travel through Route 53?

No. Route 53 only answers the lookup. The browser then opens its connection directly to the load balancer or CloudFront.

Mumbai fails. With failover routing, how long until users reach Singapore?

Two delays add up: the time for health checks to mark Mumbai unhealthy, then the TTL of the answer that resolvers have cached. With a 60-second TTL that is typically one to three minutes.

Latency routing and geolocation routing: what is the difference?

Latency sends the user to the Region that responds fastest for them, wherever they are. Geolocation sends them to the target you assigned to their country or continent, even if another would be faster. Use latency for speed and geolocation for rules.

You are releasing version 2 and want only a few users to see it first. Which policy?

Weighted. Give the version 1 target a weight of 90 and version 2 a weight of 10, watch the errors, then shift the weights.

The secondary record exists but nothing is deployed in Singapore yet. What happens on failover?

Users are sent to an address with nothing behind it. DNS failover only redirects; the second Region needs a working copy of the application and its data. Building that is disaster recovery, Day 28.

Your app servers need to find the database by the name db.internal.example.com, which must not be visible on the internet. How?

A private hosted zone associated with the VPC. Its records resolve only for resources inside that VPC.
