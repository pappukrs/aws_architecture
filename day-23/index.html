Hybrid Cloud

Most companies do not start in the cloud. They have a data centre full of systems that cannot move overnight, so for years the two must work as one network. Today you join a corporate data centre to a VPC in two ways: an encrypted VPN over the internet, and Direct Connect, a private line that never touches the internet.

The diagram

Full picture: the data centre and the VPC have different private ranges and become one routed network. Both paths end at the virtual private gateway on the VPC. The VPN sends encrypted tunnels across the public internet. Direct Connect is a physical private line through a Direct Connect location. Servers on each side reach the other by private IP address, and the app server needs no public IP.

Concepts

The two gateways

  • Virtual private gateway (VGW): the AWS end, attached to one VPC. Draw it on the VPC border, like an Internet Gateway but for private links.
  • Customer gateway: your end. It is your physical router or firewall, plus a small record in AWS holding its public IP.
  • With many VPCs, both VPN and Direct Connect attach to a Transit Gateway instead (Day 22).

Site-to-Site VPN

An encrypted connection over the ordinary internet.

  • Always two tunnels, ending in two different AZs, so AWS maintenance on one does not cut you off.
  • Ready in minutes; you only configure your router.
  • Up to about 1.25 Gbps per tunnel.
  • Speed and latency vary, because the internet in between is not yours.

Direct Connect

A dedicated physical connection from your network into AWS.

  • You or a partner run fibre to a Direct Connect location, where it is cross-connected to an AWS router.
  • 1, 10 or 100 Gbps, with steady latency.
  • Takes weeks to months to install.
  • Lower per-GB price for data leaving AWS, which matters at large volumes.
  • Not encrypted by itself. It is private, not secret.

Routing

  • The VPC route table needs the data centre's range pointing at the VGW. Route propagation adds it for you.
  • Your router needs the VPC's range pointing at the link. BGP exchanges these routes automatically and switches path when a link fails.
  • The two ranges must not overlap. A VPC of 192.168.0.0/16 could never be joined to this data centre.
  • Security groups must allow the data centre's range.

PathPublic internetPrivate fibre

The common production design is both: Direct Connect as the main path and the VPN as an automatic backup. For the highest resilience, use two Direct Connect links at two different locations.

Check yourself

VPN or Direct Connect: what are the trade-offs?

VPN is quick, cheap and encrypted, but runs over the internet, so its speed and latency vary and it tops out near 1.25 Gbps per tunnel. Direct Connect is private, fast and consistent, with cheaper data transfer at volume, but takes weeks to install, costs more, is not encrypted by default, and is a single point of failure unless you add a second link or a VPN backup.

Is traffic on Direct Connect encrypted?

Not by default. The line is private, but the data on it is in the clear. Add MACsec on the link or run a Site-to-Site VPN over the Direct Connect connection when encryption is required.

Why does a Site-to-Site VPN always have two tunnels?

For availability. Each tunnel ends at a different AWS endpoint in a different AZ. AWS can take one down for maintenance, or one can fail, and traffic continues on the other.

The app server has no public IP. Can the database server in the data centre connect to it?

Yes. Over the VPN or Direct Connect the two networks route each other's private addresses, so it connects to 10.0.2.10 directly. The security group must allow the data centre's range.

The data centre uses 10.0.0.0/16 and so does the VPC. What happens?

They cannot be routed to each other: each side thinks those addresses are local. One network has to be renumbered. Choosing cloud ranges that do not clash with existing networks is part of the very first design step.

The Direct Connect fibre is cut by road works. What keeps the business running?

A backup path. With a VPN configured alongside, BGP notices the lost routes and sends traffic through the tunnels within seconds to minutes. Without a backup, the two sites are cut off until the fibre is repaired.

The company has twenty VPCs. Do you build twenty VPNs?

No. Attach the VPN or Direct Connect once to a Transit Gateway, and attach the VPCs to that. For Direct Connect alone, a Direct Connect gateway can also share one link among several VPCs and Regions.

A migration starts next week, and the company will move 200 TB over six months. What do you set up?

A VPN now, so work can begin immediately, and order Direct Connect at once for the bulk transfer. When it arrives, make it the main path and keep the VPN as backup.
